Preventive Maintenance Checklist for Your Home's Exterior

Published 2026-04-15 by Dre Home Services LLC

The best home repair is the one you never need. Preventive maintenance costs pennies on the dollar compared to emergency repairs — and in Virginia's variable climate, skipping maintenance guarantees problems. Whether you're in Fredericksburg, Stafford, Woodbridge, or Culpeper, use this checklist to protect your biggest investment.

Spring Maintenance (March–May)

  • Roof: Inspect for loose, missing, or damaged shingles. Check flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights.
  • Gutters: Clear winter debris. Check for sagging, leaks, and proper drainage away from foundation.
  • Siding: Look for cracks, warping, or water stains. Check caulk around windows and doors.
  • Foundation: Inspect for cracks, settling, or water pooling. Ensure grade slopes away from house.
  • Deck/Patio: Check for loose boards, rot, and wobbly railings. Clean and reseal wood surfaces.

Summer Maintenance (June–August)

  • HVAC: Clean or replace AC filters. Clear debris from outdoor unit.
  • Exterior Paint/Stain: Touch up peeling areas before summer humidity worsens them.
  • Driveway/Walkways: Fill cracks before they spread in heat. Seal asphalt if needed.
  • Trees/Shrubs: Trim branches away from roof and siding (prevents storm damage and pest access).

Fall Maintenance (September–November)

  • Gutters: Clean thoroughly after leaves fall. Install gutter guards if desired.
  • Roof: Schedule pre-winter inspection. Repair minor issues before ice dams form.
  • Heating System: Service furnace or heat pump. Test before cold weather hits.
  • Windows/Doors: Install storm windows or apply plastic film. Check for drafts.
  • Exterior Faucets: Drain and shut off. Disconnect hoses to prevent frozen pipes.
  • Chimney: Have inspected and cleaned if you use a fireplace.

Winter Maintenance (December–February)

  • Ice Dams: Watch for icicles and ice buildup at roof edges. Indicates poor insulation/ventilation.
  • Interior Leaks: Check ceilings after snow/thaw cycles. Stains indicate roof leaks.
  • Foundation: Monitor for new cracks that appear in freezing weather.
  • Pipes: Keep cabinet doors open under sinks during extreme cold. Let faucets drip.
  • Snow Load: Remove excessive snow from flat roofs and decks if safe to do so.

The Cost of Neglect vs. Prevention

Clogged gutters: Prevention: $200/year cleaning | Repair: $5,000+ foundation repair

Missing shingles: Prevention: $150–$400 repair | Repair: $2,000+ decking/insulation damage

Cracked caulking: Prevention: $50 DIY | Repair: $1,500+ water damage repair
