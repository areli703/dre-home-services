Storm Damage Roof Repair: Insurance Claims Guide for Virginia Homeowners

Published 2026-04-10 by Dre Home Services LLC

Virginia sees its share of severe weather — from spring thunderstorms with hail and high winds to the occasional tropical storm remnant rolling up from the coast. If your roof is damaged, navigating the insurance claim process can feel overwhelming. Here's the step-by-step guide we give every homeowner in Fredericksburg, Stafford, Woodbridge, and beyond.

Step 1: Document Everything Immediately

Before you do anything else, document the damage:

  • Take photos of all visible damage from the ground (use a zoom lens if needed)
  • Photograph interior leaks, water stains, and damaged belongings
  • Record the date and time of the storm
  • Save local weather reports showing hail size, wind speeds, or tornado warnings
  • Make a list of all damaged items with approximate values

Don't climb on the roof yourself. Wet, damaged roofs are extremely dangerous. Leave the inspection to professionals.

Step 2: Review Your Insurance Policy

Before filing a claim, understand what your policy covers:

  • Coverage type: Actual Cash Value (ACV) vs. Replacement Cost Value (RCV). RCV pays for full replacement; ACV subtracts depreciation.
  • Deductible: Know your out-of-pocket cost before filing.
  • Exclusions: Some policies exclude "cosmetic" hail damage or have wind/hail deductibles.
  • Time limits: Most policies require claims within 1 year of damage, but sooner is better.

Step 3: File Your Claim Promptly

Call your insurance company's claims line. Have ready:

  • Policy number
  • Date and description of the storm/damage
  • Your documentation (photos, weather reports)
  • Contractor contact information (if you've already called us)

Step 4: Get a Professional Roof Inspection

This is where Dre Home Services comes in. We provide:

  • Free storm damage inspections for homeowners across Central Virginia
  • Photo documentation of every damaged shingle, dent, and leak point
  • Detailed written report with repair/replacement recommendations

Step 5: Understand the Adjuster's Estimate

Insurance adjusters use software to calculate repair costs. Common discrepancies:

  • Code upgrades: Older homes may need venting, decking, or flashing upgrades to meet current Virginia code. Insurance should cover these.
  • Matching materials: If your shingles are discontinued, insurance must pay for full replacement to ensure color match.
  • Hidden damage: Decking damage under shingles isn't visible until tear-off. We document this with photos and request supplements.

Step 6: Supplement If Needed

If the adjuster's estimate is too low, your contractor can file a supplement. At Dre Home Services, we handle supplements as part of our storm damage service. We provide additional photos, material price adjustments, code upgrade requirements, and hidden damage documentation discovered during tear-off.

Common Insurance Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting too long: Delays allow secondary damage (mold, rot) that insurers may deny.
  • Accepting the first estimate: Adjusters work for the insurer, not you. Get your contractor's independent assessment.
  • Signing over your claim: Never sign an "assignment of benefits" giving a contractor control of your insurance payout.
  • Paying upfront: Reputable contractors don't demand full payment before work. Standard is 1/3 deposit, 1/3 at midpoint, 1/3 on completion.
